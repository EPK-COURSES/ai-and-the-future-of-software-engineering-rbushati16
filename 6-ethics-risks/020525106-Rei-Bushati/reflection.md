Personal Reflection: Risks, Ethics, and Limitations of AI


What Surprised Me Most

During my research on Topic 6, what shocked me the most was the sheer volume of hidden security vulnerabilities that AI assistants can introduce into code. We often look at tools like GitHub Copilot or ChatGPT as flawless "super-programmers," but learning that they frequently replicate outdated code, bad security practices, and logical hallucinations made me realize how dangerous it is to trust them blindly.


My Concerns and Opportunities

The main question posed for this topic— What happens if engineers stop understanding the code they ship? — is deeply concerning to me as a computer engineering student. If my generation of developers relies entirely on AI to generate logic, we risk becoming code operators rather than actual engineers. If an engineer doesn't understand their code, maintaining systems or debugging a critical crash under pressure becomes almost impossible. It creates a dangerous "Black Box" where human oversight is lost.

However, recognizing these limitations also presents a huge opportunity. If I train myself to critically analyze, test, and audit AI-generated code, I can position myself as a highly reliable and secure engineer. The opportunity lies in using AI to speed up the writing of boring boilerplate tasks, while I spend my energy mastering software architecture, testing, and system security.


How I Will Adapt Personally

To protect my skills from degrading, I am setting strict boundaries for how I use AI in my academic and personal projects. I will always write the core logic of my algorithms from scratch first to ensure my problem-solving skills stay sharp. I will treat AI tools strictly as a grammar checker or a documentation assistant, never as the primary author. Finally, I will focus heavily on learning how to read code, write robust test suites, and audit security, ensuring that I fully understand and can take 100% human responsibility for every single line of code I push to a repository.
